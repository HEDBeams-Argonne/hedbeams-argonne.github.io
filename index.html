HEDBeams

High energy density beam–matter simulation

HEDBeams is a simulation effort at Argonne National Laboratory that models what happens when intense particle beams strike solid targets such as collimators and beam absorbers. Energy deposited by the beam heats the material into the high energy density regime, where it melts, vaporizes and flows. The simulations are built on the Flash-X multiphysics framework using magnetohydrodynamics (MHD) with multi-temperature physics.

About HEDBeams Flash-X

Beam energy deposition

Energy-deposition maps from particle-transport codes (MARS and FLUKA) are read in as a function of position and bunch number, and deposited into the target electrons over time.

Collimator and absorber targets

Setups for collimators and inline absorbers in materials such as aluminum and copper, including time-varying and Gaussian beam profiles and general geometries.

MHD with three temperatures

Unsplit staggered-mesh MHD with separate electron, ion and radiation temperatures, Spitzer electron–ion heat exchange, diffusion with flux limiting, and tabulated equations of state.
