About HEDBeams

HEDBeams studies the interaction of high-intensity particle beams with matter. When a beam is dumped on or scraped by a collimator or absorber, the deposited energy can raise the target far above its melting point within microseconds, producing hydrodynamic motion, ablation and magnetic-field effects that are hard to measure directly. HEDBeams provides simulations of these processes.

Physics

The simulations solve the magnetohydrodynamic equations on an unsplit staggered mesh with multiple temperatures, tabulated equations of state (for example aluminum and copper), Spitzer electron–ion heat exchange and flux-limited diffusion. A scaling factor corrects the electron–ion equilibration time in solid and liquid target material.

Beam energy deposition

Energy deposition from the beam is computed separately with particle-transport codes (MARS and FLUKA) and supplied as input files. The simulation reads these maps, interpolates the deposited energy at any position and bunch number, and applies it to the electrons of each mesh block as time advances.

Setups

The current setups cover collimators (including experiment-based and time-varying Gaussian beam cases), inline absorbers and general-geometry targets, in 2D and 3D, with AMR through PARAMESH.
